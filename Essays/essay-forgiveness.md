# Forgiveness and Its Limits

*Triad of Stewardship | Essays*

Forgiveness is a necessary virtue — until we choose to forgive the unforgivable.

This is the crossroads of moral courage and self-preservation. Forgiveness is often framed as a purely benevolent act, but when it completely dismisses accountability, it can become an act of deep personal and social injustice.

The acts many people struggle to forgive share a common thread: they violate the fundamental social contract. Violent crimes against the innocent. Abuse of power and trust. Betrayal in intimate relationships. And perhaps most practically: continued patterns of harm without genuine remorse or change. When a person repeatedly engages in harmful behavior without changing, offering forgiveness becomes enabling.

## The Moral Trap

This is the core tension: forgiveness should never be conflated with the suspension of justice or accountability.

When forgiveness removes all consequences for the perpetrator, it doesn't heal the victim; it simply clears the path for the harm to be repeated. We must be alert to the dangers of too much virtue — a virtue that costs us our well-being and allows injustice to flourish.

Signs that you may be taking a virtue to a harmful extreme:

**Sacrificing your own well-being for their comfort.** If you find yourself consistently sacrificing your own health, safety, or stability in the pursuit of "being the bigger person," you are prioritizing the offender's lack of consequence over your own necessary healing.

**Rigid application.** If you are rigidly applying a moral principle without considering the nuances of a situation, you are oversimplifying the issue and potentially causing harm to yourself or others.

**Losing perspective.** If your struggle to maintain a high moral standard makes you judgmental or self-righteous, you risk replacing compassion with moral arrogance.

Ultimately, the wisest approach to forgiveness is one that is rooted not only in empathy for the wrongdoer but in unwavering respect for the self and the pursuit of justice.
