# Death and Living Fully

*Triad of Stewardship | Essays*

Life is brief. When we're young, it seems an endless horizon, but with time and the quiet accumulation of scars, the illusion fades. So why spend our irreplaceable hours chasing what vanishes? Real joy rarely hides in wealth or titles; it waits in the stillness.

It's in the wordless ease of sitting beside someone you trust. It's a slow walk with the person whose hand still fits yours perfectly. It's the steam and bitter warmth of coffee curling through the blue light of dawn. These are not small things. They are the only things that truly etch themselves into our memory.

Death is not the adversary. Think of it instead as a quiet alarm that whispers: *pay attention.* Not later, not when the schedule clears, but right now. What we should fear far more than the end is the numbness of the middle — sliding through days without saying what matters, or postponing kindness for a "right moment" that never quite arrives.

When grief comes, try not to flee it. Let it prove that your life is real, that you are here, and that the people you love are within reach — for now.

So, let us live accordingly. Speak what is true. Love without holding back. Mend what can be mended, and gently release what has begun to poison the spirit. Stop waiting for permission to be brave, or waiting to become who you already sense you are.

And when the end arrives, there will be no need to resist. You will simply lay the story down — the way you close a well-loved book and rest it on the table, satisfied — and whisper to yourself:

*"I was here. I loved well. I tried my best."*

That is enough. It is everything.
