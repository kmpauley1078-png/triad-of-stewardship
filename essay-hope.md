# Hope as a Survival Skill

*Triad of Stewardship | Essays*

I don't want to focus on hope as if I'm looking at the world through rose-tinted glasses. I want to focus on hope because it is a practical survival skill.

Hope, like love, is not primarily a feeling. It's a choice. And it is one of the bravest choices a person can make.

## Purposeful Hope

A chosen hope is not denial. It's not slapping a smile over real pain or pretending the world isn't broken when it clearly is. It's the deliberate decision to approach life with purpose anyway — especially when everything inside you wants to give up. Bitterness often feels justified. It can even feel powerful. But it's corrosive. It drains energy, isolates the heart, and slowly convinces you that nothing matters at all.

Purposeful hope fights back. It makes no promise of easy answers or quick fixes. What it does promise is simpler and fiercer: you can still act. You can still care. You can still believe in good.

In that stubborn belief, something unexpected grows: resilience. Connection. A renewed sense of purpose.

## The Discipline of the Small

The discipline worth practicing is not passive optimism, but practiced defiance. Real work doesn't always feel joyful. Real life rarely does. Yet the deepest courage shows up precisely when we act as if joy matters anyway — when we move, speak, create, and love as though light is worth fighting for.

Because it is. Especially when everything inside us screams otherwise.

Bad things happen. And the world is still worth loving — not because the pain is meaningful, but because we are. Even then. Even now. Even here.
