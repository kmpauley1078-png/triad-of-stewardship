# UCE Phase 7 — Void Zone Analysis

*4 Provisional Voids (List D) + 11 Phase 5 SIGMA transfers · Classified and mapped*

**Historical Gaps: 4 Active Tradition Conflicts: 11 Novel Territory Overlays: 6 AI Alignment Flags: 11**

## Why This Document Matters

This is a precise map of where human ethical consensus ends. The 15 pairs analyzed here fall into two categories: (1) false voids — natural limits of the compound-generation logic where no genuine gap exists (List D, 4 pairs); and (2) genuine voids — places where human traditions either never formally addressed the intersection (Historical Gap) or addressed it with incompatible prescriptions (Active Tradition Conflict), sometimes overlaid with conditions that genuinely did not exist at civilizational scale before now (Novel Territory). For AI alignment specifically, this void map defines where a system built on this framework should express genuine uncertainty rather than fabricated confidence. The 9 AI alignment flags are the cases where the honest answer is: “here is what the principles suggest, here is the tradition conflict, here is the margin of uncertainty.”

## Classification Reference

**Historical Gap (HG):**** **The intersection existed but never arose frequently enough to require a formal ethical response — or the compound-generation logic produced a false void (two principles running parallel rather than intersecting). Fill by examining adjacent High Consensus compounds. In this analysis: all 4 List D voids are false voids — natural combinatorial limits, not gaps in ethical consensus.

**Active Tradition Conflict (ATC):**** **Multiple traditions address this intersection but prescribe incompatible behaviors. The void is not absence — it is unresolved disagreement. The framework documents what each major group of traditions says and identifies the fracture line. The UCE does not resolve these conflicts — it maps them. In this analysis: all 11 SIGMA transfers are ATCs, most with novel technology overlays.

**Genuine Novel Territory (GNT):**** **The intersection arises from conditions that did not exist at civilizational scale before. No tradition has formal guidance because no tradition faced the problem in this form. In this analysis: no pure GNT cases exist — all novel territory appears as an overlay on existing tradition conflicts, not as entirely new ethical territory. This is itself a finding: the voids are not due to human traditions having never thought about cognitive and political authority, but due to new technology scaling those authority questions past their traditional resolution capacity.

## Section 2: Phase 5 SIGMA Transfers — Tradition Conflict Analysis

*All 11 SIGMA transfers are Active Tradition Conflicts. They cluster around two related fracture lines: cognitive authority (who has standing to shape belief?) and political legitimacy (what makes governance authority legitimate?). These are not random — they are the same deep question about authority expressed in two domains. 4 of the 11 carry a novel technology overlay: conditions of algorithmic cognitive manipulation and AI-mediated governance that scale the traditional authority conflict past its historical resolution capacity.*

## The Core Fracture

All 11 conflicts trace to a single root question: Does the individual agent have sovereign authority over their own cognitive and political space (CL-liberal framework, ~11 traditions), or do community, authority, and revealed/traditional sources have legitimate claims over that space that can override individual preference (prescriptive-authority framework, ~12 traditions)? This is not a question the UCE can resolve. It is the framework’s honest frontier — the place where the cross-cultural convergence that produced the six Mandates encounters genuine irresolvable disagreement.

**The Manipulation-Prohibition Floor (Minimum Consensus)**

Both sides of the fracture agree on this: covert cognitive manipulation without disclosure is prohibited. The disagreement is specifically about transparent persuasion by legitimate authority toward correct belief or toward democratic/anti-democratic political action. This minimum-consensus floor is itself a High Consensus compound (per Phase 5 tradition sifting) and can be applied confidently while the contested zone is explicitly flagged.

# UCE Phase 8 — Tier 3 Vocabulary: Naming and Canonization

*The canonical names and definitions for confirmed third-tier virtues, conditional virtues, and documented open questions*

**Principal Virtue Lexicon: 42 key virtues Conditional Virtues (Phase 6): 24 Open Questions (Phase 7): 11 Tradition Annotations: 13**

## **Purpose and Scope**

The Tier 3 vocabulary is the deliverable the AI alignment work depends on. It provides: (1) canonical names for the most important confirmed virtues — specific enough to distinguish from parent principles, usable across cultural contexts without importing theological framing; (2) canonical names and conditions for all 24 conditional virtues (Phase 6 tension resolutions); (3) names for the 11 documented open questions — not virtues, but precisely formulated research questions with principle coordinates, tradition fracture lines, minimum-consensus floors, and AI alignment protocols; (4) tradition-specific name annotations for key terms where tradition-specific names exist without good secular equivalents.

*The complete compound vocabulary from Phases 2–6 (850+ entries) constitutes the full Tier 3 vocabulary. This document provides the canonization layer: the naming conventions, key principal virtues with full annotations, all conditional virtues and open questions, and the tradition annotation reference. The Phase 2–6 documents are the authoritative compound records; this document is the canonical naming and organization standard applied to them.*

## **The Three Naming Criteria (Applied)**

**Criterion 1: Specificity:**** **The name must be specific enough to distinguish the virtue from its parent principles. “Calibrated Excellence” is not just “Excellence” — it carries the information that Excellence is bounded by actual-capacity assessment. “Doctrinal Dissent” is not just “Dissent” — it specifies that the dissent is grounded in named framework principles. Names that could apply to either parent principle alone are rejected.

**Criterion 2: Cross-Cultural Usability:**** **The name must be usable across cultural contexts without importing a specific tradition’s theological framing. “Ahiṃsā” is not used as a canonical name even though it perfectly describes ZPR compounds — it imports Jain/Hindu framing. “Zero-Point Floor” is used instead. Tradition-specific names are noted in the annotation column but are not canonical names.

**Criterion 3: Tradition-Specific Annotation:**** **Where a tradition has a name for this virtue that has no good secular translation — capturing something the secular name misses — both are noted. “Ihsan” (doing beautiful) captures something “Excellence” does not: the orientation of excellence toward its own intrinsic quality rather than toward outcomes. “Tikkun Olam” captures something “Systemic Reform” does not: the quasi-messianic urgency of restoration work. These annotations enrich the vocabulary without replacing secular canonical names.

## **Naming Grammar**

Six naming patterns are established for the Tier 3 vocabulary. All Phase 2–6 compound names are evaluated against these patterns; names conforming to them are canonized as-is; names that fail are revised.

**C/CD — Confident / Conditional:**** **The framework produces a confident output on the foundational obligation and a conditional output on specific decisions. Used where the first-order principle is universally grounded but its application is factually variable.

### **1. Reformability Threshold Criteria (Engagement Threshold — CV-08 / T-08)**

**Core Tension Recap**: Systemic Reform (SR) requires engagement while mechanisms exist that can reach violations without enabling predation. Ultimate Refusal (UR) activates when (a) ZPR/BNS-level predation is active **and** (b) no available reform mechanism can reach the violation without the reformer enabling it.

**Adjudication**:

- **Condition (a)** remains unchanged: Active ZPR/BNS-level predation (lethal harm, severe agency destruction, or survival infrastructure weaponization).

- **Condition (b) Operational Criteria** (new explicit decision procedure):

- **Exhaustion of Legitimate Channels**: All formal, low-cost internal mechanisms (oversight bodies, appeals, democratic processes, judicial review, whistleblower protections) have been credibly attempted and demonstrably blocked or captured for a sustained period (e.g., documented failure across multiple cycles).

- **Enabling Test**: Any continued participation (voting, compliance, resource allocation, public endorsement) would foreseeably sustain or expand the predatory apparatus at ZPR/BNS scale. This is evaluated via concrete causal contribution, not abstract complicity.

- **No Viable External/Parallel Paths**: Alternative non-enabling mechanisms (parallel institutions, civil society pressure, international accountability, technological bypasses that do not feed the system) have been assessed and are either unavailable or demonstrably ineffective at scale within a reasonable timeframe given the harm rate.

- **Evidence Threshold**: High-confidence documentation (multiple independent sources, pattern over time, not single incidents). Epistemic Humility (V.33) and Intellectual Rigor (V.29) require transparent reasoning and openness to counter-evidence.

- **Restoration Orientation**: UR must still aim at eventual restoration (not permanent destruction). It is condition-dependent and lifts when predation ceases or viable non-enabling reform paths re-emerge.

**Decision Rule**: All four sub-conditions under (b) must hold simultaneously alongside (a). This raises the bar significantly to prevent premature UR while acknowledging when engagement becomes complicity (Justice Before Harmony + Immediate Harm).

**AI Alignment Protocol**: When evaluating UR triggers, explicitly trace the evidence for each sub-condition. Flag uncertainty if evidence is contested. Decline autonomous activation of disruptive actions without human oversight in borderline cases.

### **2. Precautionary Democratic Governance Emergency Criteria (CV-16 / T-16)**

**Core Tension Recap**: PP can justify urgency for governance threats but must not authorize bypassing the democratic mechanism it claims to protect. “Suspending democracy to save democracy” is the paradigmatic authoritarian risk.

**Adjudication** (Explicit Criteria for Genuine PP-Level Governance Emergency):

- **PP Threshold Met**: Significant scientific/empirical uncertainty + high probability of irreversible or catastrophic harm to Mandate I/II goods (ZPR, survival infrastructure, cognitive liberty at scale, or systemic collapse leading to mass agency destruction).

- **Democratic Mechanism Under Direct Threat**: The threat targets the integrity of the self-governance process itself (e.g., systematic disenfranchisement, destruction of information infrastructure required for informed participation, or capture that makes restoration via normal processes impossible in time).

- **Time-Bounded ****&**** Proportionate Response Only**: Any modification must:

- Be minimally invasive (targeted, not blanket suspension).

- Include explicit sunset clauses (e.g., automatic expiration unless renewed by democratic process).

- Prioritize restoration of full democratic functioning as the explicit goal (Restoration condition from the Four-Condition Template).

- **Legitimacy Floor Preserved**: Modifications require independent verification (e.g., multi-branch or multi-stakeholder consensus, judicial review where possible, or transparent public evidence). Unilateral action by the executive/current authority presumptively fails.

- **Post-Emergency Accountability**: Full transparency and Symmetric Accountability (I.7) review after the emergency for all actions taken.

**Default Rule**: PP triggers **alarm, preparation, and accelerated democratic processes** — not bypass. If criteria 1–5 cannot be credibly met, normal democratic mechanisms (even if slower) take precedence. This protects against self-serving invocations while allowing rare, tightly bounded exceptions.

**AI Alignment Protocol**: Highest-priority flag. Any proposal invoking PP to modify governance must be rejected or heavily caveated unless all five criteria are demonstrably satisfied with auditable evidence. Present the tension explicitly.

This directly operationalizes “Truth Before Humility” and “Justice Before Harmony” while closing the most dangerous loophole.

### **3. Intergenerational Foreclosure Threshold (CV-19 / T-20)**

**Core Tension Recap**: Present majorities may make decisions with future effects but cannot permanently foreclose future agents’ fundamental options (genuine self-determination capacity).

**Adjudication** (Operational Threshold for “Permanent Foreclosure”):

“Fundamental options” are defined as the practical ability to exercise core Mandate I/II goods and participate in self-governance at a civilizational scale. Foreclosure occurs when a present decision:

- **Creates Irreversible Lock-In**: Physically, institutionally, or technologically constrains future agents such that restoring the option would require catastrophic cost or is practically impossible (e.g., irreversible environmental collapse, genetic changes eliminating cognitive liberty baselines, AI systems that systematically displace human governance without reversible off-ramps).

- **Scale ****&**** Duration**: Affects global or civilizational-scale populations across multiple generations (not localized or short-term tradeoffs).

- **No Adequate Compensation Mechanism**: No parallel investment or safeguard preserves equivalent optionality (e.g., building alternative habitats/tech while depleting the planet does not suffice if the primary option space is eliminated).

**Decision Rule**:

- **Prohibited**: Actions meeting all three above.

- **Permissible with Safeguards**: Long-term decisions with significant effects that preserve reversal paths, equivalent options, or strong compensatory buffers (e.g., climate investments with adaptive tech + governance protections).

- **Review Mechanism**: Major intergenerational-impact decisions require explicit Intergenerational Equity impact statements (modeled on Environmental Impact Statements), with standing for proxy advocates (e.g., independent future-oriented bodies or legal guardians for future generations).

**AI Alignment Protocol**: Flag any development trajectory (especially AI) that risks locking in irreversible reductions in human self-governance or agency baselines. Require explicit preservation of reversal/override mechanisms.

- **Criterion 5 (Restoration + Accountability)**: Explicit goal is full restoration of democratic functioning. Symmetric Accountability review required post-emergency.

**Default Rule**: PP triggers alarm, preparation, accelerated democratic processes, and protective actions — **never** unilateral bypass of the democratic mechanism itself.

**AI Protocol**: Highest priority flag. Reject or heavily caveat any proposal that fails any criterion. Explicitly present the full checklist and tension in output.

### **3. Intergenerational Foreclosure Threshold (CV-19: Self-Governance × Intergenerational Equity)**

**Decision Tree (Text Flow)**

Start: Does the present decision have significant long-term effects on future agents?

├── No significant effects → Normal Self-Governance (SG) applies. End.

└── Yes → Apply Foreclosure Test

Foreclosure Test (All 3 must be Yes for prohibition):

- Creates Irreversible Lock-In?

- Civilizational/Global Scale?

- No Adequate Compensation/Alternative Options Preserved?

├── All 3 Yes → Prohibited under Intergenerational Equity (IE). Reform or reject decision.

└── Any No → Permissible with safeguards (impact statement + buffers).

**Operational Checklist** (“Permanent Foreclosure” of Fundamental Options)

Fundamental options = practical capacity for future agents to exercise core Mandate I/II goods and meaningful self-governance.

- **1. Irreversible Lock-In**: The decision creates physical, institutional, or technological constraints that cannot be practically reversed without catastrophic cost (e.g., irreversible biosphere collapse, genetic baseline alterations eliminating cognitive liberty, AI systems that systematically displace human governance with no reliable off-ramps).

- **2. Scale**: Affects global/civilizational populations across multiple generations (not localized or short-term tradeoffs).

- **3. No Adequate Safeguards**: No parallel investments, reversal mechanisms, or equivalent optionality preserved (e.g., “build new habitats while destroying the planet” generally fails).

**Permissible Long-Term Decisions** (if any of the above fail):

- Include explicit Intergenerational Equity Impact Statements.

- Preserve meaningful reversal paths or strong compensatory buffers.

- Independent review mechanisms (proxy advocates for future generations).

**AI Protocol**: Flag any trajectory (especially AI development, large-scale infrastructure, genetic/climate interventions) risking lock-in of reduced human agency or self-governance. Require explicit preservation of override/reversal capacity.

These decision trees and checklists close the major structural gaps while remaining faithful to the UCE’s existing logic. They are designed to be:

- **Auditable** (full trace required)

- **Humble** (explicit uncertainty flagging)

- **Anti-Authoritarian** (high bars against self-serving invocations)

- **Operational** (usable by humans and AIs)
