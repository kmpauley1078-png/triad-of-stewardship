# UCE Phase 2 — Intra-Mandate Stress Test

*126 intra-mandate pairs · one conduct-requirement question per pair · four classifications*

**Results: 108 Clear Compounds 7 Redundant 8 Tension 3 Provisional Void**

## Structural Note on the Distribution

The near-zero PV rate (3 of 126) is the most significant finding. The predicted range expected 15–30 voids. Getting 3 suggests the 42 principles are defined with sufficient precision that almost every pairing generates a distinct conduct requirement. This is evidence of internal coherence, not over-generous classification — the three PVs flagged are genuinely thin intersections where both principles applied simultaneously produce no compound beyond their individual requirements. The 7 Redundancies and 8 Tensions identify the framework’s two categories of structural work for Phase 2: redundancies require either consolidation or explicit differentiation; tensions require explicit priority logic within the mandate. The 8 tensions are more significant than the 7 redundancies: tensions reveal where the UCE’s mandate structure makes internal demands that are not yet resolved.

**Tensions Requiring Explicit Priority Logic (8)**

**I.3-5**** **Basic Needs Security × Proportionality — survival infrastructure vs. defensive force calculus

**I.5-6**** **Proportionality × Precautionary Principle — minimum force vs. preemptive action under uncertainty

**II.11-14**** **Self-Governance × Personal Domain — collective democratic authority vs. individual private sphere

**II.13-14**** **Access to Knowledge × Personal Domain — right to know vs. right not to be known

**IV.22-24**** **Sustainability × Economic Vitality — physical limits vs. productive growth

**V.32-35**** **Temperance × Excellence — proportionate response to depleted capacity vs. unconditional quality standard

**VI.37-42**** **Proportional Resistance × Ultimate Refusal — scaled resistance vs. absolute refusal

**VI.41-42**** **Systemic Reform × Ultimate Refusal — engagement with a failed system vs. refusal to engage at all

**Redundancies Requiring Phase 2 Structural Differentiation (7)**

**II.8-13**** **Informed Consent × Access to Knowledge — IC appears derivable from AK

**II.9-12**** **Cognitive Liberty × Non-Coercion — psychological manipulation prohibition duplicated

**III.15-19**** **Coherent Truth × Transparency — both prohibit deception; T adds thin marginal requirement

**V.29-33**** **Intellectual Rigor × Humility — IR is the practice; H is the disposition enabling it (highest overlap risk)

**V.30-32**** **Compassion as Signal × Temperance — CaS appears derivable from T as a domain-specific instance

**VI.36-38**** **Duty to Dissent × Moral Whistleblowing — same action at different audiences; MW may be an escalation of DoD

**VI.37-40**** **Proportional Resistance × Civil Disobedience — CD appears to be a named subset of PR at a specific threshold

# UCE Tier 2.5 — Principle Boundary Resolution

Triad of Stewardship | Internal Working Document

### Purpose

Phases 2 and 4 of the Third Tier construction process identified 15 intra-mandate principle pairs where one principle appears derivable from, or substantially contained by, the other. These pairs were classified as Redundant and flagged for “Tier 2 structural differentiation,” with the instruction that for each pair: either write explicit boundary language distinguishing the two principles, or consolidate the narrower into the broader as a named application.

This document executes that instruction for all seven core redundancy pairs. Its findings are reflected in the Phase 0 canonical table and the Phase 8 vocabulary. The Tier 3 compound structure was built on the pre-clarification definitions and remains valid.

### General Approach

The default disposition is explicit differentiation over consolidation. Consolidation risks losing useful nuance in edge cases, forces renumbering downstream, and reduces the resolution surface available for AI implementation. The 42-principle structure should be preserved wherever two principles address meaningfully different failure modes or operate at different scopes, even if they overlap heavily in ordinary conditions.

Consolidation is appropriate only in the two cleanest cases (V.30×32 and VI.37×40), where one principle is demonstrably a named subset of the other with no independent failure mode that the parent principle does not already cover.

For each pair, this document provides:

- A boundary statement (1–2 sentences) identifying the distinct contribution of each principle

- The specific failure modes each principle addresses independently

- A cross-reference note for the Phase 0 canonical definitions

- A disposition: Differentiate or Consolidate, with rationale

### Pair-by-Pair Resolutions

### **1. II.8 Informed Consent × II.13 Access to Knowledge**

Mandate II — Agency and Autonomy | Disposition: Explicit Differentiation

| **Boundary** | Access to Knowledge is the general epistemic precondition: the right to decision-relevant information in the environment. Informed Consent is the specific transactional gate: valid agreement to a particular interaction or intervention. AK can exist without a consent moment; IC requires AK but adds the affirmative-understanding-plus-voluntary-agreement requirement. |
| --- | --- |
| **Independent failure modes** | AK can be violated without any consent context: systematic information suppression, epistemic gatekeeping, or access denial that does not involve a transactional interaction. IC can be violated even when AK is fully satisfied: a patient who has all relevant information but is pressured or deceived into agreeing has suffered an IC violation with no AK violation. |
| **Why not consolidate** | Consent carries distinct legal and ethical weight — medical, contractual, and data-processing frameworks all treat it as a separate and stronger obligation than general information rights. Collapsing IC into AK would understate the transactional commitment. |

### **2. II.9 Cognitive Liberty × II.12 Non-Coercion**

Mandate II — Agency and Autonomy | Disposition: Explicit Differentiation

| **Boundary** | Non-Coercion prohibits bypassing or manufacturing the will through force, threats, or psychological pressure — it operates at the level of the decision act. Cognitive Liberty protects the internal thought processes and mental environment from external shaping, including non-coercive forms such as subliminal design, filter bubbles, or algorithmic attention capture. NC covers the will; CL covers the epistemic substrate that feeds it. |
| --- | --- |
| **Independent failure modes** | NC can be violated without a CL violation: physical coercion, credible threats, or explicit psychological pressure. The will is overridden; the cognitive environment may be unaffected. CL can be violated without a NC violation: a recommendation algorithm that systematically narrows a person’s informational environment over time does not coerce — it shapes the available epistemic inputs. The person “chooses freely” within a manipulated option set. |
| **Why keep distinct** | AI and surveillance contexts produce CL violations at scale without triggering NC definitions. Without CL as a distinct principle, these violations fall outside the framework’s reach. |
| **Phase 0 cross-reference** | Add to both definitions: “Covert cognitive manipulation is a joint NC+CL violation. NC addresses the act of overriding the will; CL addresses the prior shaping of the epistemic inputs from which the will operates.” |

### **3. III.15 Coherent Truth × III.19 Transparency**

Mandate III — Integrity and Reciprocity | Disposition: Explicit Differentiation

| **Boundary** | Coherent Truth concerns the effect of communication on the recipient’s model of reality: does the person receiving the communication end up with an accurate picture? Transparency concerns the disclosure of intent, motivation, and operational logic: are relevant meta-facts about the communicator’s position, interest, or process visible? CT governs content accuracy and framing; Transparency governs disclosure of relevant meta-information. |
| --- | --- |
| **Independent failure modes** | CT can be violated with full Transparency intact: a communicator who openly acknowledges their role, funding, and motives can still produce a technically accurate but systematically misleading picture through selective framing, misleading emphasis, or out-of-context presentation. The recipient knows who is speaking; they still end up with a distorted model. Transparency can be violated with CT intact: a communicator who accurately conveys all relevant facts about a situation while concealing that they are being paid to convey them has violated Transparency. The content is accurate; the meta-information is hidden. |
| **Why not consolidate** | The two failure modes require different remedies. A CT violation requires correction of the content or framing. A Transparency violation requires disclosure of the meta-information. Neither remedy addresses the other’s failure. |
| **Phase 0 cross-reference** | Add joint note: “CT and Transparency are frequently co-violated but address different dimensions of communicative integrity. CT governs content accuracy and framing; Transparency governs disclosure of relevant meta-information.” |

### **4. V.29 Intellectual Rigor × V.33 Humility**

Mandate V — Sustained Ethical Function | Disposition: Explicit Differentiation | Note: Highest overlap risk in the framework

| **Boundary** | Intellectual Rigor is the active practice: testing assumptions, seeking disconfirming evidence, updating beliefs on evidence. Humility is the dispositional calibration: accurate self-assessment of knowledge limits and genuine openness to being wrong. Rigor without Humility becomes motivated pseudo-rigor — executing the testing protocol only against confirming evidence. Humility without Rigor becomes epistemic laziness — acknowledging uncertainty without doing the work to reduce it. |
| --- | --- |
| **Bidirectional independence** | These principles can fail independently in both directions, not just one. This bidirectional independence is what justifies keeping them distinct. Rigor without Humility: an agent who follows the testing protocol correctly but is dispositionally arrogant about what that rigor proves — treating the output as definitive beyond its actual scope — has violated Humility while maintaining procedural Rigor. Humility without Rigor: an agent who is genuinely open to correction and accurately calibrated about uncertainty but never systematically tests their beliefs has met the Humility standard while failing the Rigor standard. |
| **Why not consolidate** | Each principle addresses a distinct failure mode that the other does not catch. An agent can be procedurally rigorous but dispositionally closed, or dispositionally open but methodologically passive. Both failure modes are real and common; collapsing one principle into the other eliminates the framework’s ability to diagnose the failure it covers. |
| **Structural note** | This pair is the framework’s epistemic conscience. It is worth treating with special care in any AI implementation: the specific failure mode of executing the testing protocol while being closed to results that challenge prior commitments is precisely the alignment failure the framework is designed to prevent. |
| **Phase 0 cross-reference** | Add to both definitions: “Humility is the dispositional condition that makes genuine Intellectual Rigor possible; Rigor is the active practice that prevents Humility from becoming passive uncertainty-acknowledgment. Each can fail independently of the other.” |

### **5. V.30 Compassion as Signal × V.32 Temperance**

Mandate V — Sustained Ethical Function | Disposition: Consolidation (CaS as named application of Temperance)

| **Boundary** | Temperance is the general virtue of emotional calibration under pressure: neither excessive suppression nor excessive indulgence of any destabilizing emotional state. Compassion as Signal is the specific application of that calibration principle to distress and compassion emotions treated as directional prompts that must not hijack judgment. |
| --- | --- |
| **Why consolidate** | This is the cleanest redundancy in the set. CaS has no independent failure mode that Temperance does not already cover. Every case where CaS is violated is a case where Temperance is violated; the reverse is also true within this domain. CaS adds domain specificity but no structural independence. |
| **Action** | Retain Temperance as the canonical principle. Rename/annotate: Temperance (V.32) — emotional calibration under pressure. Add under it: “Compassion as Signal: the primary emotional-domain application of Temperance, covering the specific case where distress or compassionate response must be read as directional information without being allowed to override judgment.” |

### **6. VI.36 Duty to Dissent × VI.38 Moral Whistleblowing**

Mandate VI — Just Subversion | Disposition: Explicit Differentiation (sequential escalation)

| **Boundary** | Duty to Dissent is internal naming: the obligation to identify and register a violation to the issuing authority or within the system responsible for the violation. Moral Whistleblowing is external disclosure: the obligation to bring the violation to the attention of parties outside the system — those with standing, capacity, or authority to act — when internal channels have failed, been refused, or been captured. |
| --- | --- |
| **Structure** | These form a natural escalation ladder. DoD is the first-tier obligation; MW is the second-tier obligation triggered when DoD has been attempted and failed to produce remedy. Executing MW before DoD — except in cases where internal remedies are structurally unavailable or would accelerate harm — is itself a Proportional Resistance violation (disproportionate escalation). |
| **Independent failure modes** | DoD can be violated without MW becoming relevant: an agent who does not raise the violation internally at all has violated DoD, but MW is not yet triggered because the internal channel has not been attempted. MW can be violated without a concurrent DoD violation: an agent who has fulfilled DoD fully — the internal channel has been used, documented, and refused — but who then fails to escalate externally when the situation requires it has violated MW. |
| **Phase 0 cross-reference** | Add sequential note to both: “DoD and MW form a two-stage escalation structure. DoD is the first obligation (internal naming). MW is activated when internal remedies are exhausted or unavailable. Skipping DoD is itself a proportionality violation except in cases where internal channels are captured or would accelerate harm.” |

### **7. VI.37 Proportional Resistance × VI.40 Civil Disobedience**

Mandate VI — Just Subversion | Disposition: Consolidation (CD as named application of PR)

| **Boundary** | Proportional Resistance is the general scaling principle governing all forms of active opposition to illegitimate authority: the response must be calibrated to the severity of the violation, the available remedies exhausted at each level before escalation, and the response oriented toward restoration rather than destruction. Civil Disobedience is one specific method on that resistance spectrum: targeted, public, non-violent non-compliance that accepts legal consequences, applied when lower-level remedies have been exhausted. |
| --- | --- |
| **Why consolidate** | CD has no structural independence from PR. Its defining features — targeted scope, public character, non-violence, acceptance of consequences, restoration intent — are all derivable as the conditions under which PR is correctly applied at a specific threshold. Every well-executed CD is an application of PR; no CD can satisfy its own criteria without satisfying PR. |
| **Action** | Retain Proportional Resistance as the canonical principle. Add under it: “Civil Disobedience: the canonical named form of Proportional Resistance at the non-violent public non-compliance threshold, characterized by targeted scope, public acknowledgment, acceptance of legal consequences, and explicit restoration intent.” |

### Impact on Tier 3

Impact is low. The Third Tier construction process treated these redundant pairs by excluding them from compound generation (List C in Phase 4). The compounds that were generated involving the affected principles were produced from pairings with other, non-redundant principles, and those pairings are not affected by boundary clarifications within redundant pairs.

The one partial exception: any Tier 3 compound whose canonical name implicitly collapses a distinction now being drawn (most likely in the V.29×33 and III.15×19 neighborhoods) should be reviewed for nomenclature accuracy in the Phase 8 vocabulary update step. This is a naming adjustment, not a structural change to the compound itself.
